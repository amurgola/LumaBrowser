# Sweat, hygiene and smell

Grounding cards for sweat by cause and area, and for how a body changes between freshly washed and days unwashed.

## Sweat by cause and area
Tags: *
When: sweat, sweating, sweats, sweaty, armpits, ran, running, fought, fighting, exertion, panting, flushed
Unless: running late, running out, ran out, running a meeting, running the, ran the, fighting over, flushed the
- Fresh sweat is almost odourless and tastes of salt; the smell comes from skin bacteria working on it over the next half hour to hours.
- Work or heat sweat: thin and watery, beads on the brow and lip, runs down the spine and chest, soaks the back of a shirt; dries to white salt lines on dark cloth and leather.
- Fear or stress sweat: from the armpits, groin, palms and soles; thicker and more pungent, smelling sharper and sooner than exercise sweat; clammy palms, a cold trickle down the ribs.
- By area: armpits strongest and muskiest; groin and between the buttocks musky and sour; feet cheesy or like vinegar inside closed boots; chest and back mostly salty; scalp oily.
- Diet and drink come through: garlic, onions, alcohol the morning after, curry spices.
- Dried sweat in clothes goes stale and sour, worse each time it gets wet again.

## Freshly washed
Tags: showered, bathed, washed, freshly washed, just bathed, clean, from washing, hair wet from washing
When: *
- Smell: soap, oil or whatever was used, or simply warm clean skin; the person's own faint scent underneath.
- Skin: flushed and warm from hot water, or tight and dry from harsh soap; fingertips pruned after more than a few minutes in water; damp hair darker and heavier.
- It fades: after a few hours of work, heat or fear, the body's own smell is back.

## Unwashed for days
Tags: unwashed, filthy, grimy, days without washing, not washed, reeks, jungle, on the march, campaign, in the field, imprisoned, captive, castaway, homeless
When: *
Unless: washed, from washing, bathed, freshly washed, scrubbed clean, cleaned up, after a bath, after a shower, showered
- Day one to two: greasy hair and face, oily skin, armpit odour noticeable up close, stale breath in the morning.
- Day three to five: hair lank and clumped, scalp itching; a film on the skin; armpits, groin and feet smell strong and sour; teeth furred, breath stale all day; clothes stiff and reeking; skin rubbed raw where straps, seams and thighs chafe.
- A week and more: lice, flea bites, sores, rashes and fungus in the folds, worst in heat and damp (jungle rot on the feet, crotch rot); others smell them from a step away or more.
- Heat and humidity speed all of it; cold slows it.
- Noses go blind: people stop noticing their own smell and that of companions in the same state; a clean stranger notices at once.
- Fiction gets wrong: lovely shining hair and fresh breath after a week in the wild.

## Breath
Tags: *
When: breath, breathe, breathing, kiss, kisses, kissed, kissing, mouth, yawn, lean in, leans in, whisper
- Morning or after sleep: stale and sour; after long thirst or fever: dry, thick and foul.
- After vomiting: acid, sour and bitter; after drink: sweet and sour at first, then stale and coming through the skin the next morning.
- Long hunger or uncontrolled diabetes: fruity, like nail polish remover.
- Bad teeth or a mouth infection: rotten, putrid; smoking: ashtray; garlic or onions: for a day.
- Freshly cleaned teeth, mint, wine or tea leave their own taste.

## Intimacy and the state of the body
Tags: *
When: kiss, kisses, kissing, kissed, embrace, embraces, intimate, undress, undresses, naked, bed, bedroll, make love, making love, caress, sits close, leans her head, leans his head
- Freshly washed: skin smells of soap or of the person; skin tastes faintly salty at most; hair soft; breath matches what they last ate or drank.
- After exertion or a long day: salt on the skin, musk at the neck, armpits and groin, a damp warmth; many people find a little of this appealing, a lot of it off-putting.
- Days unwashed or ill: strong armpit and groin odour, stale breath, oily or matted hair, grime in skin folds, chafed or rashy skin; a partner notices at once, and the person usually knows it and is self-conscious or apologetic.
- The body under arousal or nerves: flushed face and chest, quicker breathing, more sweat, warm skin; afterwards skin sticky and cooling.
- Practical life: people wash, rinse their mouths, or laugh about it; in hard places (camps, ships, prisons) privacy and water are scarce and that shapes what happens.
- Fiction gets wrong: two people after a week in a swamp meeting as if fresh from a bath.
