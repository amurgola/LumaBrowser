# Hands, skin and smell up close

Grounding cards for hands, skin and the smell of a body when someone is looked at closely or touched.

## Working hands
Tags: dockworker, labourer, mechanic, farmer, builder, fisherman, sailor, huge hands, smith
When: hands, hand, palms, palm, fingers, knuckles, gloves, towel, grip
- Look: broad, thick fingers; yellow calluses across the top of the palm and the pads, from rope, tools or handles; old white scars and nicks on the knuckles; nails short, thick, split or blackened; grime or grease in the creases and cuticles that washing does not shift.
- Touch: rough, dry and hard, a sandpaper handshake; strong grip even when tired.
- Fiction gets wrong: a lifetime of hard work leaving soft, unmarked hands.

## Wheelchair user's hands
Tags: wheelchair, wheelchair user, manual wheelchair, paraplegic, push rims
When: hands, hand, palms, palm, fingers, gloves, grip
- Look: calluses on the heels of the palms and along the thumbs from the push rims; gloves worn thin in the same places; strong forearms.
- Touch: firm grip, rough palms; the hands get cold and filthy on wet ground because they touch the wheels.

## Old hands
Tags: ageAdvanced, elderly, frail, old woman, old man, arthritis, arthritic, swollen knuckles
When: hands, hand, fingers, knuckles, palms, palm, grip, mug, cup
- Look: thin skin that shows the tendons and blue veins; brown age spots; purple bruises from the smallest knocks; with arthritis, knobbly finger joints, fingers drifting sideways at the knuckles, a ring that no longer comes off.
- Touch: cool and dry, light as paper; slow, careful grip; they wrap both hands around a warm mug.

## Hands after cold and wet
Tags: soaked, wet, drenched, flood, river, rain, cold, frozen
When: hands, hand, fingers, fingertips, knuckles, palms, palm, towel, gloves, mug, cup
- Look: fingertips white and wrinkled; knuckles red and raw; nails bluish; rope burns as shiny red stripes across the palm of anyone who hauled a line.
- Feel: stiff, clumsy and numb, then burning, throbbing and itching as they warm; buttons and laces are beyond them for a while.

## The smell of a body after a hard night
Tags: soaked, wet, drenched, flood, river, unwashed, filthy, grimy, sweaty, sweat-soaked, sick, feverish, vomiting, exhausted, battle, fought, jungle, smoke, smoky
When: smell, smells, stink, stinks, reek, reeks, socks, boots, hug, hugs, embrace, embraces
- What carries: wet wool and wet coats, river or flood water (mud, rot, sometimes diesel or sewage), sour sweat; fear sweat smells sharper and more metallic than exercise sweat; smoke clings to hair and wool.
- By body: a child smells of little but whatever they rolled in; an old person of soap, powder or mothballs in their clothes; a heavy worker of sweat and tobacco or grease.
- How noses work: a smell hits on first contact or when a boot or coat comes off, then fades within a minute or two as the nose adjusts; strangers notice it, the person rarely does.
- Keep it light: one plain note of smell is enough; it is not a comment on the person.

## Skin up close
Tags: *
When: skin, shoulder, neck, sleeve, sleeves, rolls up, bare back
- By age: a child's skin is smooth and quick to flush or bruise; a young adult's marked by acne scars, tan lines or freckles; an old person's thin, creased, spotted and quick to bruise and tear.
- After a hard night: gooseflesh, a blue tinge at the lips and nail beds in the cold, red pressure marks from straps and seams, scrapes not yet noticed.
- Lasting marks worth giving a body: a scar with a story, a tattoo, a birthmark, freckles, a vaccination dent, stretch marks; once named, keep them.

## How an ordinary person smells up close (any body)
Tags: *
When: sniff, sniffs, sniffing, smell, smells, smelling, scent, nose, nuzzle, nuzzles, inhale, inhales
Unless: smell trouble, smells trouble, smell a rat, sniffs at the idea
- Layers, strongest first: what they work with or just handled (coffee, cooking, machine oil, paper, soil), then soap, shampoo or laundry, then the skin itself, warm and faintly salty.
- Where it gathers: hair and the collar hold it longest; hands smell of the last thing they held; clothes of the room they spent the day in.
- An animal's nose reads far more than a person's: other animals, food eaten hours ago, where the shoes have been.
- Keep it light: one or two plain notes, nothing that judges them.
