# Blood and wounds

Grounding cards for bleeding, severe wounds, shock and death. Moment cards apply when the player's line names the
character and does something the When line lists; standing cards (When: *) apply every turn the character's tracked
condition matches their Tags.

## Fresh blood from a wound
Tags: *
When: cut, cuts, wound, wounded, blood, bleeding, bleeds, stab, stabbed, slash, slashed, gash, arrow, bullet, shot, bite, bitten, nosebleed, axe, sword, blade, hacked, hacks
Unless: cuts through, cut through, cutting through, cuts in, cut in, cuts across, cut across, cut short, cuts off, cut off the engine, shortcut, short cut, haircut, blood orange, bloody mary, blood moon, blood pressure, sword fish, swordfish, shot glass, espresso shot, a shot of, bite to eat, quick bite
- Look: from an artery bright red and pulsing out in time with the heartbeat, sometimes in a jet; from veins darker red and welling steadily; spread over skin, cloth or a floor it always looks like more than it is.
- Touch: warm, then sticky and tacky within minutes; slippery on hands, hilts and reins; dries to a brown-black crust that cracks, flakes and itches; it stiffens cloth.
- Smell and taste: metallic, like coins or iron; a lot of it in a closed space hangs heavy and sweetish.
- Small cuts clot in a few minutes; scalp and face cuts bleed far more than their size; pressure stops most bleeding.
- Fiction gets wrong: clean dabs of blood, or wiping it off with one pass.

## Severed or nearly severed limb
Tags: severed, cut off, hacked off, lost his arm, lost her arm, lost his hand, lost her hand, lost a leg, amputated, stump, lopped
When: *, stump, severed, hacked off, cut off, amputate, amputated, burned shut, sear, seared
- The blow: taking a limb through the bone needs a heavy, well placed edge; most battle wounds cut deep and stop at the bone, leaving the limb hanging by muscle and skin.
- First seconds: adrenaline often masks pain; many feel a heavy thump or a burning, look down, stare, and only then understand; some keep moving for a few steps, few keep fighting.
- Bleeding: a severed main artery (upper arm, thigh) can empty a person in minutes; torn vessels sometimes clamp down and bleed less than expected; pressure, a tight strap or tourniquet above the wound, or fire to sear it are what stop it.
- The stump: ragged meat, the white or pink end of bone, muscle drawn back from the cut; it throbs, then burns; the missing hand may still feel present.
- Searing: a hot iron leaves black, blistered flesh and a smell of burnt meat and hair that people remember for life.
- Fiction gets wrong: fighting on for minutes, or dropping dead on the spot every time.

## Blood loss and shock
Tags: bleeding heavily, blood loss, lost a lot of blood, bleeding out, haemorrhage, hemorrhage, shock, pale and bleeding
When: *
Unless: bleeding stopped, bleeding has stopped, no longer bleeding
- A little (a cup or two): barely noticed; a little dizzy, heart quicker.
- More (about a fifth of their blood): pale, cool, clammy skin, a fast thin pulse, anxious and restless, thirsty, breathing quick.
- Serious (about a third): grey or ashen, confused, sweating cold, gasping, cannot stand without fainting, lips pale, nail beds slow to pink when pressed.
- Near death (about half): drowsy or unconscious, barely breathing, skin cold and waxy; then the heart stops.
- After: if they live, days of weakness, dizziness on standing and a craving for water and salt.
- Fiction gets wrong: a man who has bled a puddle walking, talking and joking as if nothing happened.

## Infected wound
Tags: infected, festering, wound gone bad, pus, gangrene, rotting wound
When: *, infected, festering, pus, gangrene, bandage stinks
- After two or three days: the edges red, hot, swollen and hard; throbbing pain that gets worse instead of better; red streaks creeping up the limb; a fever with chills.
- Pus: thick yellow, green or brownish; a sweet, rotten smell when the dressing comes off.
- Gangrene: skin turning grey, purple, then black; blistering; crackling under the skin in gas gangrene; a smell of rot that fills a room; without cutting it away or modern medicine it kills.
- Fiction gets wrong: wounds that heal clean in the open, in dirt, without care.

## Death and the body
Tags: dead, killed, dying, corpse, slain, died
When: *, corpse, dead body
- At the moment: the body goes slack, the jaw drops, eyes usually stay part open and glaze; the bladder and bowels often let go, so death smells of urine and faeces as well as blood.
- Minutes to hours: skin pales to grey; blood settles to the lowest parts as purple blotches (from about half an hour, fixed after about eight hours); the body cools about a degree an hour.
- Stiffening: the jaw and eyelids first after two to six hours, the whole body by about twelve, easing again after a day or two.
- A battlefield: blood, opened guts (sour and rank), voided bowels, smoke, churned mud, flies within the hour in warm weather.
- Fiction gets wrong: eyes closing on their own, and clean, quiet, odourless deaths.

## Belly wound
Tags: gut wound, belly wound, stomach wound, gutted, disembowelled, stabbed in the gut, stabbed in the belly
When: *
- At first: may bleed less outside than expected; deep pain, the person curls around it, cannot straighten or breathe deeply.
- Smell: a pierced gut lets out a sour, faecal stink that others notice.
- Course: without surgery, infection spreads through the belly over a day or two: a hard, swollen, rigid belly, fever, vomiting, thirst; it was the slow death of every pre-modern battlefield.
- Fiction gets wrong: a gut stab that is shrugged off with a bandage.
