# Fluids, excretion and illness

Grounding cards for saliva, tears, vomit, urine, faeces, menstruation and the body under fever and gut illness.

## Saliva and the mouth
Tags: *
When: spit, spits, spat, saliva, drool, drooling, mouth, lips, swallow, swallows, thirst, thirsty
- Fear and thirst dry the mouth: tongue sticky, lips cracked, saliva thick and ropy, hard to swallow or speak.
- Nausea floods it: the mouth waters thinly just before vomiting.
- Sleep or unconsciousness: drool at the corner of the mouth, a damp patch on the pillow or sleeve.
- Spat blood: bright red and frothy from the lungs, darker and mixed with spit from the mouth or nose; dark brown like coffee grounds from the stomach.
- Dried saliva on skin smells stale.

## Tears and a running nose
Tags: *
When: cry, cries, crying, cried, tears, sob, sobs, sobbing, weep, weeping, nose, sniff, sniffs
- Crying hard: the nose runs and blocks, eyes swell and redden, the face goes blotchy, breath comes in hitches and hiccups; afterwards a headache and puffy lids for hours.
- Cold air or smoke: eyes water and noses drip without any emotion.
- A cold: clear watery snot that thickens and yellows over days; sneezing, sore red nostrils.

## Vomiting
Tags: *
When: vomit, vomits, vomiting, threw up, throw up, throws up, retch, retches, retching, puke, nausea, nauseous, seasick, brings it back up, bring it back up, brought it back up, brings it straight back up, sick up
- Before: pale, sweaty, mouth watering, swallowing hard, the world tilting; then retching that bends the whole body.
- What comes up: partly digested food and drink, sour and acidic; once the stomach is empty, yellow-green bitter bile; burns the throat and nose.
- After: shaky, teary, cold-sweated, a sour taste and smell that lingers on breath, hair and clothes; thirsty but likely to bring water back up.
- Smell sets others off: people gag at the smell and sound of it.
- Fiction gets wrong: a single tidy heave and instant recovery.

## Urine and the bladder
Tags: *
When: pee, pees, peed, peeing, piss, pissed himself, pissed herself, wet himself, wet herself, urinate, urinated, urine, bladder, relieve himself, relieve herself, chamber pot
- Colour and smell: well watered, pale straw and nearly odourless when fresh; dehydrated, dark amber and strong; stale urine in cloth or bedding turns sharply of ammonia over hours.
- Need: cold, fear, coffee, alcohol, pregnancy and old age all make people need to go more often; holding it becomes a nagging ache, then pain.
- Terror can make people lose control of the bladder; afterwards warmth turns quickly cold and clammy, and it chafes.
- Fiction gets wrong: characters who never need to stop on a long march, siege or stakeout.

## Faeces, bowels and latrines
Tags: *
When: shit, shits, poop, bowels, bowel, toilet, latrine, privy, squat, squats, diarrhoea, diarrhea, dysentery, cramps, soiled
- Ordinary: brown, a strong smell that fills a small room; most people go once or twice a day and hate an audience.
- Fear: the bowels can loosen in terror or just before battle.
- Camps, ships, sieges: latrines, pits and buckets, flies, the stink downwind; dirty water and hands spread gut illness through a group within days.
- Fiction gets wrong: armies, travellers and prisoners with no toilet at all.

## Gut illness (diarrhoea, dysentery, food poisoning)
Tags: diarrhoea, diarrhea, dysentery, food poisoning, the runs, flux, cholera, stomach bug, gut sickness
When: *, diarrhoea, diarrhea, dysentery, the runs
- Cramps that come in waves, sudden urgent need, watery and frequent stools; in dysentery blood and mucus, in cholera pale rice-water in huge amounts.
- Dehydration fast: dry mouth, sunken eyes, dark little urine, headache, dizziness on standing, weakness; a child or an old person can be in danger within a day.
- The body: pale, sweaty, smelling sour and sick, sore raw skin from wiping; no appetite; days of weakness after.
- What helps: water with salt and sugar, rest, shade, clean water.
- Fiction gets wrong: dysentery as a joke or a one-scene inconvenience.

## Fever and sickness
Tags: fever, feverish, flu, malaria, infection, chills, delirious, burning up, burning hot, shaking with chills
When: *, fever, feverish, burning up, burning hot, shaking with chills, chills, delirious
Unless: fever broke, fever has broken, fever gone, no fever
- Rising: cold, shaking chills even under blankets, teeth chattering, while the skin is already hot.
- Peak: hot, dry, flushed skin, glassy bright eyes, a pounding headache, aching joints, fast pulse; at high fever confusion, strange dreams or delirium.
- Breaking: drenching sweats that soak bedding and smell sour and sickly; then exhausted and weak for days.
- Malaria: chills, fever and sweats come in cycles every day or two.
- Fiction gets wrong: a fever that ends with the hero waking clear-eyed and strong the next morning.

## Menstruation
Tags: *
When: period, menstruating, menstruation, monthly bleeding, cramps, her courses, time of the month
- Bleeding for three to seven days, heavier in the first two; dark red to brownish, sometimes with small clots; the total is a few spoonfuls to a small cup.
- Body: cramping low in the belly and back, bloating, tender breasts, tiredness, sometimes nausea or headaches; some people are barely affected.
- Practical: pads, cloths, cups or tampons, changed several times a day; a faint metallic smell only when they cannot be changed; in hard places (march, prison, wilderness) managing it is a real daily problem.
- Fiction gets wrong: periods that never happen to anyone, or that turn a capable person helpless.
