# Feet and legs up close

Grounding cards for what shows when boots, shoes and socks come off or trouser legs are rolled up. A card applies
when its When line matches what the player is doing to a named character this turn, and its Tags match that
character (description, looks or condition); Tags: * means any body.

## Feet after hours in wet footwear
Tags: soaked, wet, drenched, flood, river, rain, waded, sodden, jungle, swamp, marsh
When: boots, boot, socks, sock, shoes, trainers, slippers, wellies, stockings, feet, foot, toes, barefoot
- Look: soles and toes white, wrinkled and puckered like a washerwoman's hands; the sock weave pressed into the skin; toenails bluish; the tops pale, then blotchy red as they warm.
- Touch: cold and clammy, the skin soft and spongy, easy to rub raw or tear; a blister burst and gone white at the heel.
- Feel: numb at first, then pins and needles and a deep burning ache as blood comes back; tender to stand on.
- Smell: wet wool, rubber or leather, river mud, sour sweat that blooms when the boot comes off and fades after a minute as noses adjust.
- After many hours: feet swollen, mottled and numb, then painful for days (trench foot); they need drying, warming slowly and rest, not rubbing.

## Working feet (big, heavy, years in boots)
Tags: dockworker, labourer, mechanic, farmer, soldier, work boots, sizeVeryTall, very tall, huge, big man, builder
When: boots, boot, socks, sock, feet, foot, toes, barefoot
- Look: broad, long feet with high arches flattened by the load; thick yellow calluses on the heels, the balls and the outside of the big toe; thick, ridged toenails, one may be black or missing from a dropped weight years ago; hair on the toes; a red band where the boot top sat.
- Touch: hard skin that scratches, cracks on the heels.
- Smell: a day or a night in boots, strong and cheesy at first.
- Fiction gets wrong: a labourer's feet as clean and smooth as anyone else's.

## Old feet
Tags: ageAdvanced, ageOlder, elderly, frail, stooped, old woman, old man
When: slippers, stockings, shoes, socks, feet, foot, toes, barefoot, ankles
- Look: thin, shiny, nearly hairless skin over bones and blue veins; bunions at the base of the big toe; toes bent like claws or crossed over each other; thick, yellowed, ridged toenails; dry, cracked heels; old bruises in yellow and purple.
- Touch: cold, slow to pink up when warmed; skin that tents when pinched and tears like tissue if pulled.
- With arthritis: knobbly swollen toe joints, stiff when moved.
- Keep them a person: they may be embarrassed to be seen and brisk about it.

## Child feet
Tags: ageChild, ageYoungChild, ageToddler, schoolboy, schoolgirl, small for his age, small for her age
When: wellies, boots, shoes, socks, sock, trainers, feet, foot, toes, barefoot
- Look: small, narrow, pink and soft; dirt in the creases between the toes; scabs and bruises on shins and ankles; a red rubbed patch or blister on the heel or ankle from wellies that are too big.
- Moves: wriggles, curls the toes, cannot sit still to be looked at; ticklish.
- Cold: go white and wrinkled fast in wet boots, then bright red and hot when warmed, and the child says they itch and burn.

## Swollen feet in late pregnancy
Tags: heavily pregnant, eight months pregnant, nine months pregnant, seven months pregnant, swollen ankles, pregnant
When: trainers, shoes, socks, slippers, feet, foot, ankles, ankle, legs, barefoot
- Look: puffy feet and ankles, the ankle bones lost in the swelling; skin tight and shiny; deep red lines where laces, sock bands and shoe edges pressed; the feet may look a size bigger than they are.
- Touch: pressing a thumb in leaves a dent that fills back slowly; warm rather than cold.
- Feel: heavy, tight and achy; better with the feet up; she cannot easily reach or see her own feet.
- Watch for: sudden swelling of the face and hands, a bad headache or blurred vision is a warning sign that needs a medic now.

## Legs and feet after spinal cord injury
Tags: paraplegic, paraplegia, spinal injury, spinal cord, wheelchair user, T10, T12
When: legs, leg, trousers, trouser legs, socks, sock, shoes, feet, foot, toes, ankles, shins
- Look: calves and thighs thinner than the arms and shoulders suggest, the muscle wasted to soft; feet slightly swollen, pale or bluish and mottled from hanging down in the chair; marks pressed in by sock bands, shoe edges or the footplate.
- Unfelt damage: a graze, bruise, blister or burn he never noticed, which is why people check by eye; cold feet can be frostnipped without any pain.
- Moves: a leg may jump, twitch or stiffen in a spasm when touched or cold; he lifts and positions his own legs with his hands.
- Keep him a person: he knows the routine, does the checking himself and says what he needs; he is matter of fact about it.

## Legs up close (any body)
Tags: *
When: leg, legs, calf, calves, shin, shins, knee, knees, ankle, ankles, trouser leg, hem
- Through cloth: what meets a hand, a shoulder or an animal's flank is the fabric first (denim stiff and cool, wool rough, a skirt hem moving, tights smooth) with the warmth of the leg coming through it a moment later.
- The leg itself: firm at the calf, bone close under the skin at the shin and ankle; standing people shift their weight and the muscle moves under the cloth.
- Smell at that height: washing powder and the day's wear on the cloth, floor dust, whatever they walked through or work with (coffee, oil, grass, an animal at home).
- Reaction: people notice contact at the leg at once; they go still, step back or look down before they decide what to do about it.
- Keep it small: one detail of cloth, warmth or smell is the whole moment.

## Feet, socks and shoes on an ordinary day (any body)
Tags: *
When: shoe, shoes, sock, socks, foot, feet, toes, laces, heel, heels, sole, soles, sandal, sandals, sneakers, trainers, boots, boot
- Shoes: leather, rubber or canvas, and the street on the soles (dust, wet pavement, cut grass); worn shoes crease across the toes and tread down at the outer heel.
- Socks and feet after a normal day: faintly warm and a little damp, a mild sour-salt smell under the shoe's own; stronger after hours standing or in closed shoes, close to nothing in sandals or fresh socks.
- Who notices: a nose at floor level (a dog, a cat, a child) gets it at once; the person does not notice at all and other adults only when a shoe comes off.
- Keep it plain and brief: a fact of the day, never a comment on the person.
