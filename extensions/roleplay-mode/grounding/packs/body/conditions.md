# Ongoing conditions

Grounding cards for common long term conditions that change what a body can do in a hard moment.

## Asthma
Tags: asthma, asthmatic, inhaler, wheezy
- Triggers: cold air, exertion, smoke, dust, damp and fear can set off an attack.
- Attack: tight chest, wheeze, cough, short sentences between breaths; sitting up and leaning forward helps; the reliever inhaler works within minutes.
- Danger: no inhaler, getting worse despite it, too breathless to speak, blue lips, or going quiet and drowsy means a serious attack.
- Fiction gets wrong: asthma that never matters during a run through smoke.

## Type 1 diabetes
Tags: diabetic, diabetes, insulin, type 1, blood sugar
- Low blood sugar (after effort, missed food or too much insulin): shaky, sweaty, pale, irritable, confused, can look drunk; needs fast sugar (sweets, juice) at once, then food.
- Very low: slurred speech, cannot follow instructions, seizure or collapse.
- High blood sugar builds over hours (thirst, peeing a lot, tiredness), not minutes.
- Fiction gets wrong: needing insulin in an emergency when the real danger is usually too little sugar.

## Burn scars and skin grafts
Tags: burn scars, burn scar, scarred, skin graft, burned
- Moves: thick or grafted scar over joints is tight and limits stretching (reaching overhead, bending the fingers); scars can pull and tear with sudden stretching.
- Skin: scarred skin does not sweat, so the person overheats; it can be numb or itchy, and burns easily in sun or near heat.
- Fiction gets wrong: scars as only a look, with no effect on movement or temperature.

## Chronic pain and fatigue conditions
Tags: chronic pain, fibromyalgia, chronic fatigue, long covid, lupus, flare
- Energy: a fixed and small budget; a hard effort today is paid back with a crash over the next day or two.
- Pain: varies day to day; adrenaline can carry them through a crisis, then the cost arrives hours later.
- Fiction gets wrong: invisible conditions treated as weakness of will.

## Adult sex differences (averages only)
Tags: averageSex
- On average, adult men are about 13 cm taller and much stronger in the upper body; women have about half to two thirds of men's upper body strength and closer to three quarters in the legs.
- The ranges overlap a lot: a fit woman is stronger than many men, and individual training, size and age matter more than sex.
- Women's hands and feet cool faster in cold, but overall endurance and cold survival are similar.
- Fiction gets wrong: either ignoring size and strength in a physical contest, or making it the only thing that matters.
