# Mobility and limbs

Grounding cards for impairments of movement and the ways people adapt to them.

## Paraplegia (lower spinal cord injury, wheelchair user)
Tags: paraplegic, paraplegia, spinal injury, spinal cord, wheelchair, wheelchair user, T10, T12, L1, manual wheelchair
- Body: no movement or feeling below the level of injury (around the belly button for T10); arms and hands work fully; trunk balance is partial, so they brace with one hand when reaching or on a rocking surface.
- Strength: years of pushing and transferring build strong shoulders, arms and grip; they lift their own body weight with their arms many times a day.
- Moves: transfers chair to seat or floor with their arms; on stairs, many sit on a step and lift themselves up or down backwards one step at a time with their arms while someone brings the chair; being carried works best with two people or as they direct.
- Legs: can spasm or jerk on their own; cannot feel cold, wet, cuts or pressure there, so injuries and frostnip in the legs go unnoticed until someone looks.
- Cold: sweating and shivering below the injury are reduced, so they chill (and overheat) more easily.
- Keep them a person: the chair is their legs; do not grab or push it without asking; they know how to be moved and expect to be asked.
- Fiction gets wrong: "confined to a wheelchair", weak arms, being lifted like a child, or never having a practical answer for stairs.

## Leg amputation (with or without a prosthesis)
Tags: amputee, amputation, prosthetic leg, prosthesis, artificial leg, one leg, missing leg
- Moves: walks well on a fitted prosthesis on even ground; slopes, wet surfaces, stairs and ladders take care; running needs a running blade; crutches or hopping without it.
- Costs: walking uses noticeably more energy, especially above the knee; the socket chafes and sweats on long days.
- Water: most everyday prostheses must not get wet; swimming is done without it.
- Sensation: phantom sensations or pain in the missing limb are common, worse with stress and cold.
- Fiction gets wrong: a prosthesis that behaves like a real leg in every way, or a person who cannot move without one.

## Arm or hand amputation
Tags: one arm, missing hand, missing arm, prosthetic arm, hook hand, amputated arm
- Moves: does most daily tasks one handed with practised tricks (teeth, knees, the stump, pinning things against the body); two handed grips, ropes and ladders are hardest.
- Balance: losing an arm changes balance a little, and falls cannot be caught on that side.
- Fiction gets wrong: helplessness, or a prosthetic hand with full grip and feeling.

## Bad knees (arthritis, old injury, braces)
Tags: bad knees, bad knee, knee brace, knee braces, knee injury, wrecked knees, dodgy knee, knee surgery
- Moves: going down stairs and slopes hurts more than going up; kneeling, crouching and getting up from low seats need a hand to push from; a knee can give way under load or on a twist.
- After: swells and aches after effort; stiff in the morning or after sitting; worse in cold and damp.
- Hides it: many people walk off the pain and refuse help until the knee buckles.
- Fiction gets wrong: injuries that only matter in one scene.

## Arthritis in the hands
Tags: arthritis, arthritic, swollen knuckles, gnarled hands, rheumatoid, stiff fingers
- Hands: stiff and sore, worst in the morning and in cold; swollen knuckles; weak pinch and grip; fine work (buttons, knots, keys, jar lids, buckles) is slow and painful.
- Holding on: a sustained grip on a rope, rail or boat side cannot be held long; they hook with the forearm or palm, use both hands, or wedge themselves instead.
- Pain: a firm handshake or a pulled hand hurts; they offer the forearm or elbow.
- Fiction gets wrong: arthritis as only an old person's mild ache with no effect on what the hands can do.

## Chronic back pain or back injury
Tags: bad back, back injury, back pain, slipped disc, sciatica
- Moves: stiff and careful, avoids twisting and bending; lifts with the legs or not at all; long standing or sitting in one position builds pain.
- Flare: a wrong move can lock the back in spasm, leaving the person unable to straighten or walk normally for hours or days.
- Fiction gets wrong: shrugging off a back injury to carry someone.

## Walking aids (cane, crutches, walker)
Tags: cane, walking stick, crutches, walker, zimmer frame, limp
- Moves: a cane goes in the hand opposite the weak leg; on stairs it is one step at a time with a rail; crutches leave no hands free and are slow and tiring on stairs and slopes.
- Hazards: wet, icy or uneven ground, loose rugs and dark stairs; a dropped cane is hard to pick up.
- Fiction gets wrong: aids as props that are forgotten when the action starts.
