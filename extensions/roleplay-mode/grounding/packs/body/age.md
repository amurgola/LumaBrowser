# Body by age

Grounding cards for how age shapes a body in a scene. Each card is one retrievable unit: the Tags line is what a
character's description must mention for the card to apply (and is never shown to the narrator).

## Toddler (1 to 3)
Tags: toddler, infant, ageToddler
Group: age
- Moves: wide unsteady gait, falls often and gets up; climbs onto things but not down; must be carried any distance or over rough ground; an adult carrying one has only one free arm.
- Limits: cannot follow more than a one step instruction; no sense of danger; grabs and puts things in the mouth.
- Cold and heat: chills and overheats very fast; wet clothes in wind are dangerous within minutes.
- Under stress: screams, clings, goes rigid or limp; picks up fear from the adults' faces and voices more than words.
- Fiction gets wrong: toddlers sitting quietly through a crisis, or speaking in full sentences.

## Young child (4 to 7)
Tags: young child, little girl, little boy, ageYoungChild
Group: age
- Moves: quick bursts, then tires and wants carrying; poor at judging gaps, heights and traffic; can squeeze through spaces no adult can.
- Limits: weak hands, short reach; carries only light things; cannot swim reliably even after lessons.
- Cold and heat: loses heat much faster than an adult; lips go blue and teeth chatter early, then they go quiet, which is the warning sign.
- Under stress: freezes, hides, cries, or asks the same question again and again; obeys a calm adult with one short instruction at a time.
- Keep them a person: curious and brave in bursts; notices odd things adults miss.
- Fiction gets wrong: small children reasoning like adults, or forgetting hunger, cold and tiredness.

## Child (8 to 12)
Tags: schoolboy, schoolgirl, small for his age, small for her age, ageChild
Group: age
- Moves: fast, light and agile; climbs well, fits through hatches and gaps; about 25 to 40 kg, so an adult can lift and boost them easily.
- Limits: roughly a third to half an adult's strength; cannot hold a rope with their own weight for long, cannot drag an adult, tires on long walks.
- Cold and heat: small body, so cold water or wind chills them far faster than the adults around them; shivering hard, then clumsy fingers, then slurred talk and quiet.
- Under stress: wants a job; bravado, chatter or sudden tears; fear of the dark and of being left behind is real at this age.
- Keep them a person: competent at small physical tasks, proud of being useful, sharp at noticing.
- Fiction gets wrong: children who are either helpless props or as strong as an adult.

## Teenager (13 to 17)
Tags: teenager, teen, adolescent, ageTeen
Group: age
- Moves: near adult size by the end, often gangly during growth spurts; quick and recovers fast from exertion.
- Limits: strength still building, especially before 15; may overestimate what they can do; needs a lot of sleep and food.
- Under stress: swings between capable and childlike; hides fear behind anger, jokes or silence; peers matter more than adults.
- Fiction gets wrong: teens as small adults with no growth, sleep or hunger needs.

## Older adult (60 to 74)
Tags: retired, grey haired, ageOlder
Group: age
- Moves: still capable and often fit, but slower to start and to recover; stiff after sitting or sleeping; balance a little worse on uneven ground.
- Limits: less muscle and power than at 40; joints and back complain after lifting or kneeling; reading glasses for close work; hearing loss in noise is common.
- Cold and heat: tolerates both a little worse; dehydrates more easily.
- Keep them a person: endurance for steady work is often good; skill and judgement are at their peak.
- Fiction gets wrong: sixty treated as frail, or as no different from thirty.

## Advanced age (75 and over)
Tags: elderly, old woman, old man, frail, stooped, grandmother, grandfather, cane, walking stick, walker, ageAdvanced
Group: age
- Moves: slow, short steps (about half a young adult's pace), wide stance, reaches for rails and furniture; stairs one at a time, leading up with the stronger leg and down with the weaker; stands up in stages and may get dizzy rising fast.
- Limits: weak grip, thin skin that bruises and tears easily; a fall can break a hip or wrist; tires quickly and recovers slowly, often over days.
- Cold and heat: feels cold sooner but shivers less, so chills dangerously without seeming to; dehydrates and overheats easily.
- Senses: needs far more light; glare, dark and low contrast hide steps and edges; high voices and fast speech in noise get lost.
- Keep them a person: the mind, wit and experience are intact; they plan around their limits and hate being handled.
- Fiction gets wrong: elders who either collapse helplessly or keep pace with the young.
