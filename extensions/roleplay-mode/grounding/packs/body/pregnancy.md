# Pregnancy

Grounding cards for a pregnant body across the trimesters.

## Early pregnancy (first trimester, to about 13 weeks)
Tags: early pregnancy, newly pregnant, first trimester, few weeks pregnant
Group: pregnancy
- Body: usually not visible; nausea (any time of day), smell sensitivity, deep tiredness, sore breasts, needing to pee often.
- Moves: full mobility and strength; may get lightheaded if hungry, hot or standing still long.
- Fiction gets wrong: a visible bump in week six, or constant dramatic vomiting.

## Mid pregnancy (second trimester, about 14 to 27 weeks)
Tags: pregnant, pregnancy, expecting, second trimester, five months pregnant, six months pregnant, bump
Group: pregnancy
- Body: a visible bump; energy often returns; the baby's movements are felt; back and round ligament twinges with sudden moves.
- Moves: still very mobile; balance begins to shift forward; lying flat on the back starts to cause dizziness.
- Fiction gets wrong: treating mid pregnancy as fragile; most people work and move normally.

## Late pregnancy (third trimester, about 28 to 40 weeks)
Tags: heavily pregnant, eight months pregnant, nine months pregnant, seven months pregnant, third trimester, pregnant
Group: pregnancy
- Moves: centre of gravity is forward, so she leans back and walks with a wider gait; cannot see her feet on stairs; getting up from low seats or the floor takes a push with the arms or a roll to the side.
- Breath: the womb crowds the lungs, so stairs and hurrying leave her breathless; she needs to pause and lean on something.
- Limits: should not lift heavy loads; climbing, jumping down or squeezing through gaps is risky because a fall onto the bump is the danger; she lowers herself and slides rather than drops.
- Comfort: back ache, swollen ankles, heartburn, needing to pee often; lying flat on her back makes her faint or nauseous, so she lies on her left side.
- Heat: runs warm; may tolerate cold better than others but tires faster.
- Labour signs: practice tightenings (Braxton Hicks) are irregular and fade with rest; real labour contractions come at regular intervals, get closer and stronger, and do not stop; waters breaking is a gush or a steady trickle.
- Keep her a person: still capable, decisive and strong willed; she knows her body and gives instructions.
- Fiction gets wrong: going into labour from a shock and delivering in minutes, or being helpless.
