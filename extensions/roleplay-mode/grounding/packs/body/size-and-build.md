# Body by size and build

Grounding cards for height, mass and conditioning. Ranges, not rules: every card describes what is typical and what a
scene should account for.

## Very tall and heavy (around 195 cm and up, 120 kg and up)
Tags: very tall, huge, enormous, giant, massive, barrel chest, huge hands, big man, sizeVeryTall
Group: height
- Strength: high absolute strength; can lift and carry another adult briefly, best over the shoulder or with the person's arms around his neck; a cradle carry in the arms lasts seconds, not minutes.
- Costs: moving that mass is expensive; stairs, ladders and climbing leave him breathing hard and sweating, and his knees and back take the load; he heats up fast and cools slowly.
- Space: shoulders over 55 cm wide and a deep chest do not fit small windows, hatches or crawl spaces; he has to go diagonally, one arm and shoulder first, and may simply not fit; low beams and door frames catch his head.
- Others: floors, ladders and small boats rock or creak under him; where he steps in a boat matters.
- Fiction gets wrong: giants as tireless and nimble, or squeezing through gaps that fit average people.

## Short or slight adult (under about 155 cm, or very light)
Tags: short, petite, tiny, slight, small woman, small man, slender, sizeShort
Group: height
- Moves: shorter stride, so has to walk faster to keep up; reach falls short of high shelves, hatches and handholds; fits tight spaces others cannot.
- Strength: lifts and carries less in absolute terms, though can be strong for their size; cannot easily move a heavy unconscious adult alone.
- Cold and heat: less mass, so cold water and wind chill them sooner than larger people.
- Fiction gets wrong: small characters overpowering big ones in a straight contest of strength.

## Dwarfism (achondroplasia)
Tags: dwarfism, achondroplasia, little person, short limbs
Group: height
- Body: average sized trunk and head with short arms and legs; adult height around 120 to 135 cm; elbows may not fully straighten.
- Moves: walks well but with a shorter stride and tires on long distances; climbing high steps, ladders and vehicle steps takes effort; uses step stools and adapted reach.
- Health: back and knee pain and spinal narrowing are common in adults; strength is normal for the muscle they have.
- Keep them a person: full intelligence and ordinary adult capability; they are adults, not childlike, and expect to be treated so.
- Fiction gets wrong: comic relief, magic, or being picked up and moved by others.

## Heavy body (obesity)
Tags: obese, fat, heavyset, overweight, very heavy, portly, stout
- Moves: steady on flat ground but breathless sooner on stairs, hills and running; standing up from low or soft seats and from the floor takes effort.
- Limits: knees, hips and back carry more load; tight spaces and some seats, belts and harnesses do not fit; bending to reach the feet is awkward.
- Cold and heat: holds heat well, so overheats with exertion but handles cold water longer than a lean person.
- Keep them a person: often strong, since daily movement is weight training; fitness varies widely at the same size.
- Fiction gets wrong: heavy characters as lazy, slow witted or only a joke.

## Very thin or underweight
Tags: gaunt, skinny, emaciated, bony, underweight, wiry
- Limits: less reserve; cold bites fast and shivering starts early; tires sooner without food; sitting or lying on hard surfaces hurts the bones.
- Wiry, not starved: a lean, wiry person can be fit, quick and strong for their weight, with good endurance.
- Fiction gets wrong: thin always meaning weak, or starving people having normal energy.

## Muscular, strength trained
Tags: muscular, broad shoulders, muscled, bodybuilder, strongman, powerful arms, brawny
- Strength: lifts, pulls and carries far more than average, especially with the upper body; grip lasts longer on ropes and ledges.
- Costs: heavy muscle burns energy and oxygen; big efforts are short, and sprinting or swimming far tires them; less flexible through the shoulders.
- Fiction gets wrong: unlimited strength with no breathing, sweat or shaking muscles afterwards.

## Fit for endurance (runner, swimmer, hiker)
Tags: athletic, runner, swimmer, hiker, fit, lean, endurance
- Moves: keeps a steady pace for hours, recovers breath quickly, climbs stairs and hills without stopping.
- Limits: not necessarily strong at lifting; a lean build chills faster in cold water.
- Fiction gets wrong: fitness covering every skill; a runner is not automatically good at climbing or carrying.

## Unfit, sedentary adult
Tags: unfit, out of shape, sedentary, desk job, soft
- Moves: fine for normal life; breathless after a couple of flights of stairs or a short run; muscles burn and shake after sustained effort.
- After: sore and stiff for one to three days after unusual exertion.
- Fiction gets wrong: ordinary people sprinting, climbing and fighting for long stretches without flagging.
