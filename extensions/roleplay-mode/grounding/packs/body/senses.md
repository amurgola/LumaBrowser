# Senses

Grounding cards for sight and hearing differences.

## Cataracts and low vision
Tags: cataract, cataracts, cloudy eyes, poor eyesight, low vision, failing eyesight, nearly blind, thick glasses
- Sight: blurred, dim and washed out, as through a fogged window; colours fade toward yellow; faces at a distance are blurs.
- Light: bright light and headlights glare and halo; dim light is far worse than for others, so dark stairs, steps and edges vanish.
- Moves: depth and contrast are poor, so stairs, kerbs and the gap between a dock and a boat are misjudged; they feel with the foot or cane and want a hand on a rail.
- Coping: recognises people by voice and walk; keeps things in fixed places.
- Fiction gets wrong: seeing details across a room, or being totally blind.

## Blindness
Tags: blind, blindness, guide dog, white cane, sightless
- Sight: most blind people have some light or shape perception; few see total black.
- Moves: confident in known places with a cane or guide dog; new or changing places, crowds, moved furniture and loud noise (which masks echoes and cues) are hard.
- Guiding: they take the guide's elbow and walk half a step behind; they want to be told about steps, gaps and doorways before reaching them.
- Senses: pay closer attention to sound, touch and smell, but do not have superhuman hearing.
- In the dark: when the lights go out, they are the most at home of anyone.
- Fiction gets wrong: touching faces to know people, or supernatural senses.

## Deaf or hard of hearing
Tags: deaf, hard of hearing, hearing aid, hearing aids, signs, sign language, partially deaf
- Hearing: hard of hearing people lose high pitched sounds and consonants first; speech in noise, wind or rain becomes mush; hearing aids pick up noise as much as voice and are often ruined by water.
- Lipreading: catches only part of speech and needs light and a face turned toward them; in darkness it fails completely.
- Deaf signers: sign language is a full language; they need to see the hands, so darkness cuts communication off; touch and light become the channel.
- Fiction gets wrong: reading every word from lips, or hearing perfectly when the plot needs it.
